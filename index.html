Clinical intersection

The reference guide organizes food decisions around kidney function, urate/gout, and blood-pressure considerations. Check the food’s details and your own lab-based care plan before making changes.

Clinical caveat: Use this guide as a reference, not an individual prescription. Potassium, phosphorus, protein, sodium and fluid needs can differ with laboratory results, medicines, and clinician guidance.

Nephrology principles

CKD Stage 4 · eGFR 15–29

The source guide covers potassium, phosphorus additives (“phos”), protein portions, and sodium as kidney nutrition topics. It also includes “Allowed / Low-Toxin Foods” and “Critical Strict Avoidances” sections.

Allowed / lower-toxin foodsUse the guide’s food reference as a starting point; confirm serving sizes and restrictions with your care team.
Foods flagged for strict avoidanceReview the guide’s rationale and product ingredients. The same food can vary by preparation and brand.
The guide contains specific numeric targets. Do not apply a fixed potassium, phosphorus, protein, or sodium limit to yourself unless your nephrologist or renal dietitian has advised it.
Rheumatology principles

Gout & hyperuricemia

The guide discusses organ meats, some small fish, beer, and high-fructose corn syrup as flare-related food topics, and includes sections on uric-acid-lowering / neutral foods and high-purine triggers.

Uric acid lowering & neutral foodsReview the listed examples and the rationale in the full source.
High-purine & flare triggersPortion, frequency, medicines, and individual response all matter.
Food choices do not replace prescribed urate-lowering treatment. Discuss food changes and gout medicines with your clinician.
Vascular & kidney considerations

Hypertension

The guide includes blood-pressure supportive foods and high-sodium / vasoconstrictor topics. It notes that standard DASH-style high-potassium foods may need individual review with Stage 4 CKD.

Antihypertensive & vasoprotective foodsUse as a discussion prompt with your nephrologist or dietitian.
Vasoconstrictors & high sodiumReview labels and consider sodium per serving in the context of your care plan.
Do not change blood-pressure medicines or start a restrictive diet based on this reference alone.
